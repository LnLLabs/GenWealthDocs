# The problem

In crypto, those who control the private key or seed phrase of a wallet have complete control over the cryptocurrency held under that seed phrase and can spend it at their discretion. That is why it is so important to keep your private key safe, offline, and not shared with anyone.

From the moment someone else has access to your seed phrase, they will be able to spend all your crypto and will have as much right to it as you.

If you do not lose your private key and if you do not share it with anyone, you will be safe using crypto and have a better experience than with traditional finance.

However, even if you do everything right, there is a drawback to this self-custody model: what happens to your crypto once you die, or if you lose your private key and cannot access it anymore?

Your crypto can be lost forever. You lose the wealth and potential gains you accumulated. If you die without providing your family with a way to recover your crypto, that value can be impossible for them to access.

This is a huge problem that every blockchain faces. Blockchains have been missing practical on-chain recovery and inheritance, and large amounts of cryptocurrency are lost forever — harming user experience and long-term confidence in these networks.
